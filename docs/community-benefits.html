Data center community benefits plans

If a data center is proposed near you, use these drafts to put requests in writing before the vote. Start with the one that fits your community.

Download a PDF to share or a text file to edit. Each includes the sample agreement. For tax incentives and permits, see the county and state drafts.

Sample agreement

Project: [site and phases]. Parties: [landowner], [operator], [guarantor] and [community signatories]. Replace the brackets with local names, amounts and deadlines before anyone signs.

Resident representation
Residents choose their representatives at an open meeting. Include renters and neighbors outside the tax district. The developer pays for interpretation and an independent adviser from a separate account. Publish the draft for [review period] and respond publicly to suggested changes. Residents may participate and still oppose the project.
Payments to the community
The operator pays [fund holder] [amount] when [milestone] occurs, then [amount] each year, adjusted for inflation using [index]. Residents hold a majority of seats on the board that awards grants. The board publishes its conflict-of-interest rules; the treasurer posts receipts and spending every quarter. List taxes, tax breaks, infrastructure reimbursements and gifts separately from the promised payments.
Who can enforce each term
Put payments in the signed agreement, land-use restrictions in the permit and electricity charges in the approved utility terms. Ask local counsel who has authority to sign and enforce each one. Any right for residents to enforce the agreement must be written into it. The agreement cannot replace environmental review, public hearings or Tribal consultation, or change treaty rights.
Missed payments and complaints
Residents choose an independent monitor who can inspect invoices, meter readings and payroll with names removed. The operator deposits [annual monitoring budget] through closure. Each quarter the monitor publishes what was due, paid, late or disputed, with the supporting records. Residents can call [phone] or file with [office] online. The office acknowledges complaints within [days] and publishes outcomes without names. After written notice, the operator has [days] to fix a breach. Specify who may draw on [financial guarantee] if it does not, who decides disputes and where either party can appeal.
Sale or closure
Before construction, the owner provides a financial guarantee for the independently estimated cost of restoring the site. Update the estimate and guarantee every [interval]. Release the original owner only after a buyer accepts the obligations in writing and supplies replacement security. List payments that remain due if work stops early. Publish the signed schedules, amendments, annual audits and final inspection.

Payment and inspection record

Make a copy for each obligation:

  • Work promised: [starting condition], [target and units], [deadline].
  • Payment: [who owes it], [budget and source], [amount due], [amount paid], [recipient and date].
  • Inspection: [who checks the work], [public record], [next review date].
  • If the operator falls short: [who gives notice], [time to fix it], [remedy and who can use it].

For home repairs, for example, the budget shows what the city approved. A paid invoice and a final inspection show which repairs were finished. Record both.

Before the vote, read out any unresolved terms. Attach the payment and inspection records to the agreement. Record who signed, the vote date and where residents can get a copy.

Rural county

A well owner may need a water test before excavation; a volunteer fire district may need equipment before the first truck arrives. Involve both, along with farmers and the water supplier. Approach affected Tribal governments separately, on their own terms.

Well testing and water limits
The operator pays for an independent lab chosen by [water district and resident representatives]. Offer well owners a depth and water-quality test before digging; keep home locations private. For each water source, specify the daily peak and annual limits, expected consumption, discharge quality, sampling points and cuts during drought. Keep daily readings. Publish monthly totals, the highest-use day, water returned, the drought stage and any violations or repairs. The monitor checks the daily logs. If [trigger] occurs, the operator supplies drinking water and pays for an independent investigation. Agree in advance what evidence will require a permanent repair.
Road repairs and fire equipment
The road superintendent and fire chief estimate the cost of road repairs, training and equipment. The developer deposits [amount] before construction. Inspect haul roads and bridges before and after truck traffic. If the developer leaves damage unrepaired after written notice and the agreed deadline, the county may use the repair guarantee.
Local training and farm internet service
Residents decide how to spend [annual amount] from the benefits fund. They might pay for trades training or better internet service for farms. The board publishes awards, eligibility and receipts every year. For training, specify [paid places per class] and [wage]; report how many people finish and find jobs. Name the fund holder who can collect an overdue payment under the agreement.

In August 2026, Salix, Iowa, voted down a proposed pause; a local organizer asked for both town and rural residents to be heard. That vote did not approve a campus. In New Mexico, Doña Ana County’s signed Project Jupiter agreement separates water commitments from scheduled payments. Its guarantee covers specified unpaid amounts; it does not guarantee damages for every breach.

Mason County, Kentucky: school payments per building

The county’s agreement summary sets supplemental annual school payments at $2 million per building. Its $15 million public-safety fee is included in the infrastructure commitment. The summary gives the amounts due. It does not say which payments the county has received.

Ask the school board to publish a building-by-building schedule: what starts the annual payment, what is due if construction stops, and which deposits cleared. Do not count the safety fee a second time.

Malaysia offers another way to check water use. Its data center incentive guidelines call for tracking water use against the electricity used by computing equipment. Ask for the meter total as well as the ratio: a larger campus can use less water per unit of electricity and still draw more from local wells.

Growing town or suburb

If the developer builds only the first phase, who pays for the larger water main the town already ordered? Have the finance director, school district and utility review those costs with residents, including people in newly annexed areas.

Taxes and school funding
Before the incentive vote, the finance director shows the town’s budget with and without each phase: taxes, tax breaks, school and fire revenue, staffing and debt payments. Each district checks its figures. Before the town releases an installment it controls, verify [permanent jobs], [wages] and [investment]. Set out how much the town can recover for a shortfall, if local law permits, and how the developer can appeal.
If the developer cancels
The operator pays for [road and water works] as the engineer signs off on each stage. Ask the utility for its approved terms for grid upgrades, deposits, minimum bills and exit fees. The utility or its regulator sets electricity charges. Before the town places an order, the developer guarantees the costs the town cannot recover if a phase is late or canceled. Name who can collect, revise the guarantee as costs change and credit anything the town can reuse.
Annual payments and local jobs
Set a minimum annual payment per operating building. Give the first due date and the amount owed if no more buildings open. The operator funds [paid places per class] at [wage] through the college. The college reports enrollment, completions and jobs six months later. Use payroll totals to distinguish years of construction work from permanent jobs. If places go unfilled, the operator funds replacement places by [date].

Marshalltown’s August 2026 ordinance included substantial expansions and recognized vested rights. Inventory pending applications before projecting growth. A separate Cedar Rapids amendment pairs qualifying jobs per phase with a fund of up to $18 million over 20 years. The cap is neither an annual payment nor proof of receipt.

Adamstown, Maryland: the county declined the deal

On September 14, Frederick County’s executive rejected the developer’s agreement application and extended the pause on new applications. The announced benefits package was not money secured for residents.

Ask the developer to list the approvals it wants in exchange. Residents should be able to see those terms before the county decides whether to sign.

In Norway, registering a data center with the regulator does not give it permission to build. Ask the town clerk which approvals a developer has in hand before the town budgets for new revenue.

Dense urban neighborhood

People who live along the truck route or across from a generator will hear it from home. Let tenants and neighbors choose who speaks for them, and give people who work evenings a way to take part.

Noise at homes and schools
Before permits are issued, the operator pays a noise specialist chosen by residents to take daytime, nighttime and low-frequency readings at agreed homes and schools. Include existing traffic and industrial noise. Write the limits, hours, locations and test methods into the permit or agreement. Publish results when the campus opens and each month thereafter. Set a deadline to fix and independently retest a verified breach. Send permit violations to [agency].
Generator tests and complaints
Publish the list of permitted generators, scheduled tests, emergency rules and school contacts. Log testing hours separately from emergency runs. Independent monitors publish their readings and explain what they can measure. Residents can report noise or fumes without proving a violation. The operator responds in writing within [days]. Refer emergencies to the air regulator immediately.
Paid training for neighbors
Residents choose a training partner. Specify [paid places per class], [wage] and [budget], including transit or childcare. Publish eligibility and outreach rules. Report enrollment by service area and, after six months, completions, jobs, wages and retention without naming trainees. The independent fund holder checks fees and wages. If the operator misses its target, it pays for another class. Residents choose the remaining grants at a public meeting.

In January 2026 the New Orleans council reported a temporary hold while planners studied zoning, sound mitigation and a possible one-year interim district. In Lancaster’s agreement, developers must provide a $10 million letter of credit at construction financing. Ask who may draw on security; a credit instrument is not cash received by residents.

South Memphis, Tennessee: tax revenue for home repairs

On November 13, the city reported a $3,259,282 approved allocation under Ordinance 5953, including $2 million for home repairs in the five-mile zone around Colossus I. The money comes from property taxes. The city had approved the repair budget; it did not report how many homes had been repaired.

Publish four columns: money collected, budget approved, awards made and repairs completed. Identify which residents qualify and how someone without internet access can apply.

Multi-campus corridor

Several campuses may draw from the same watershed, use the same substation or send trucks down the same road. Ask the neighboring towns, utility and downstream water users to review the costs together.

One map of all planned phases
For each parcel and phase, list the owner, tenant, expected opening date, peak electricity demand and water needs by source on the busiest day and over a year. An independent regional engineer publishes totals for built, delayed and canceled phases. Show metered use separately from capacity reserved. Neighboring communities review the map; each permitting authority still decides applications in its own jurisdiction.
Who pays for shared roads, pipes and substations
For each upgrade, the operators and asset owner sign a schedule naming who pays what share and how the owner will check the finished work. Before ordering equipment, each operator guarantees costs that cannot be recovered if it pulls out. Name who can collect and subtract the value of anything the owner can sell or reuse. Submit grid charges, deposits and exit fees to the utility for approval through its usual process. Publish the decision, except for protected information.
Payments across town lines
Each operator pays [amount per operating phase] to an independent fund. Upstream, downstream and host communities have seats on the board. Publish how the money will be divided before the first payment. Audit each operator and phase annually; count a shared grant or job only once. If a tenant leaves, the party that signed still owes the money until the fund accepts the agreed replacement guarantee.

Tulsa’s March 2026 permit pause excepted the first two phases of Project Anthem. The Tulsa site record includes the operator’s plans and community responses. List what each phase can already do. Google’s company plan addresses direct grid connection costs; it does not establish how every upstream upgrade will be paid for. For a different kind of commitment, Wisconsin’s commission announced a 15-year minimum term for We Energies’ very large customer rate in April 2026. That local decision does not establish actual household bill impacts.

Tallaght, Ireland: apartments on the heat network

South Dublin County Council says its heat network began serving university and public buildings in 2023 and expanded in 2024 to include 133 cost-rental apartments. The council reports which buildings receive heat. It does not say what each household saved.

In Odense, Denmark, the operator reported that heat deliveries had begun in 2020. Its annual figure for the expansion was a forecast. Ask for a meter reading from the heat customer, the cost of the pipes and the name of the party who pays if either site closes.

If your community has a moratorium

Ask the clerk for the adopted text, amendments, effective and end dates, application map, and exemptions for existing approvals. A proposal, an advisory vote and an enacted pause have different effects. A dated tracker entry does not establish that a pause is still in force.

During a pause, use only the activities the text permits. Ask the authorized body to publish outstanding studies, resident objections and what remains unresolved before its next public decision. Compare a smaller project, delay, relocation and no project. A private agreement cannot lift a public restriction.

Find the jurisdiction in the tracker’s Moratoriums directory.

Sources

For a public meeting, these guides can help residents decide which documents to request. Regional examples were reviewed November 15, 2026.

  • NAACP: Stop Dirty Data — publish project impacts before bargaining over money; residents need time and independent advice.
  • American Rivers: Data Centers and Rivers — ask separately about water withdrawn, water returned, discharge quality and who pays for new capacity (pp. 7–10).
  • World Resources Institute: Powering Progress — distinguish who signs from who benefits; design training that gives workers options beyond a single campus.
  • Federation of American Scientists: finding eight agreements — ask the clerk for executed contracts and exhibits; a promise to file a later plan leaves terms unsettled.
  • Science for Georgia: community benefit agreements — name the person who measures compliance and the party able to enforce each requirement.

Compare these proposals with Microsoft’s company-wide commitments on power, jobs and taxes. Ask who will sign for the local campus, how much is due and when. None of the organizations listed here has endorsed these drafts.
